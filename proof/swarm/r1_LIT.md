### A. erdosproblems.com/902 and forum thread (fetched 24 Oct 2026)
URLs: https://www.erdosproblems.com/902 and https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/902
- Statement: f(n) = min order of a tournament in which every set of n vertices is dominated by another vertex. Credited to Schutte (early 1960s, asked of Erdos). Status: listed OPEN.
- Listed bounds: f(1)=3, f(2)=7; Erdos [Er63c]: 2^{n+1}-1 <= f(n) << n^2 2^n; Szekeres-Szekeres [SzSz65]: f(3)=19 and n 2^n << f(n). Refs on page: Er63c, Er82e, SzSz65, OEIS A362137, teorth/erdosproblems, formal-conjectures 902.lean. (The page lists NO value or bound for f(4); nothing about skew-Hadamard/Paley.)
- Forum (4 comments, as summarised by the fetch tool; each comment is short): (1) epassports, 26 Aug 2026: Lean4/Mathlib formalisation of 2^{n+1}-1 <= f(n) and f(n) <= n+3n^2 2^n, plus f(1)=3, f(2)=7 (github.com/jaredwilder/erdos902). (2) Alfaiz 14 Dec 2025: cites Graham-Spencer, "A constructive solution to a tournament problem", Canad. Math. Bull. (weaker, explicit). (3) StijnC: GS paper does not solve the problem; gap factor n. (4) Alfaiz agrees.
- Conclusion: the erdosproblems page does NOT contain f(n) >= (n+2)2^{n-1}-1 (it lists the weaker 2^{n+1}-1 from Erdos and the asymptotic n2^n << f(n)), and nothing on f(4). It does not pre-empt our results.

### B. Jeffries, "Schutte's property for sets of tournaments and an application to dice games", arXiv:2604.08790 (9 Apr 2026, math.CO; Iowa State). READ in full via read_paper.
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08790
- Definition 1.1: T has S_k if every k-set has a vertex dominating it; f(k) = least order.
- Theorem 1.1 (quoted from the paper, attributed to Erdos 1963 and Szekeres-Szekeres 1965): (1) f(k) <= min{n : C(n,k)(1-2^-k)^(n-k) < 1} <= (log 2 + O(1)) k^2 2^k  [paper writes 2^k C(n,k) hmm: text is garbled; the standard Erdos bound has no extra 2^k]; (2) f(k) >= 2^{k+1}-1 (Erdos); (3) f(k) >= (k+2)2^{k-1}-1 for k>2 (Szekeres-Szekeres).
- Paper's own words: "The bounds in Theorem 1.1 remain the best known bounds for f(k)"; Conclusions: "The bounds on f(k) have remained un-improved for 60 years."  => as of April 2026 NO improvement of SS lower bound is claimed in the literature this author knows; this pre-dates and does not contradict our f(n) >= (n+5/2)2^{n-1}-1.
- "Szekeres and Szekeres ... considered tournaments where every k set of vertices is dominated by at least m other vertices [SS65]. They conjectured that their lower bound is the true value for f(k)."  (secondary-source statement; I could not access the original Math. Gazette paper.)  NOTE: SS lower bound at k=4 is 6*8-1 = 47. Our f(4) >= 52 therefore REFUTES that conjecture at k=4 (if our proof is right). SS conjecture holds for k=1,2,3 (3,7,19). So our result is not pre-empted; it is a counterexample to the SS conjecture.
- Small values: "P_3, P_7, P_19 are S_1,S_2,S_3 ... equal to the lower bound. According to Bozoki, Fisher showed computationally that P_67, P_331, P_1163 are the smallest S_4, S_5, S_6 Paley tournaments." Table 7 of the paper gives upper bounds f(4) <= 67, f(5) <= 331, f(6) <= 1163 (Paley). Also: "f(5) >= 111" (this is (5+2)2^4-1 = 7*16-1 = 111, arithmetic checked).
- No lower bound for f(4) beyond 47 is cited. No exact value for f(4). So the literature (to Apr 2026) has 47 <= f(4) <= 67.

### C. Simon, "Minimum Tournaments with the Strong S_k-Property and Implications for Teaching", arXiv:2205.08357 (17 May 2022, cs.DM). READ in full.
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.08357
- States (his Section 3): 2^{k-1}(k+2)-1 <= f(k) <= min{n: C(n,k)(1-2^-k)^{n-k} < 1} <= (1+o(1)) ln 2 k^2 2^k; "the gap between the lower and the upper bound is of order k". Lower bound credited to Szekeres-Szekeres.
- Theorem 4.2 (Graham-Spencer 1971): the QR-tournament of order p (p = 3 mod 4 prime) has weak S_k provided p > k^2 2^{2k-2}. Theorem 4.3 (Simon): same bound gives the STRONG S_k property (all 2^k sign patterns). Proof uses Burgess/Weil-type bound |sum_x prod_i chi(x-a_i)| <= (r-1) sqrt p. (Hence Paley-type 3-dominator results are only the generic character-sum bound: k=3 needs p > 9*2^4 = 144; k=4: p > 16*2^6 = 1024. No sharp small-p theorem.)
- Strong S_k: F(k) = least order with all sign patterns; F(k) >= f(k). The lower bound argument cited is unchanged.
- Nothing about f(4) exact.

### G. Reid, McRae, Hedetniemi, Hedetniemi, "Domination and irredundance in tournaments", Australas. J. Combin. 29 (2004), 157-172. READ (PDF https://ajc-new.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/29/ajc_v29_p157.pdf, extracted locally).
Note: their domination number gamma(T) > k  <=>  T has S_k.
- They state (their eq. (2)): (k+2)2^{k-1}-1 <= f(k), attributed to Szekeres-Szekeres [17]. Quote of Graham-Spencer [7] (as reproduced): "QRT7 has S_2 and QRT19 has S_3. In [17] it is shown that f(2)=7 and f(3)=19 ... Also, it is true that QRT67 has property S_4. Since (2) gives f(4) >= 47, it is possible that QRT67 is also minimal."
- Their Theorem 5 / proof: smallest tournament with gamma=4 (i.e. S_3) has 19 vertices (indegree >= 9 argument; this is the Szekeres argument for k=3). Verified by hand that QRT19 has no dominating triple.
- Fisher (private communication 1996), verbatim: "the smallest ROTATIONAL tournament with domination number 5 is QRT67" (i.e. smallest circulant S_4 tournament has 67 vertices), "the smallest QRT_p with domination number k has p=331 if k=6, p=1163 if k=7", "smallest QRT_p with domination number 8 has p >= 3079" (domination number 6,7,8 = S_5,S_6,S_7). Not independently refereed (private communication).
- Theorem 2 there (Tyszkiewicz 2000): from T with S_k on n vertices one gets W on n^3 vertices with S_{ceil(3k/2)}-ish ("S_{3k/2}") (explicit, size poly in 7^{3^m}: much worse than Erdos).
- Lu-Wang-Wong 2000 (cited there): "minimum number of stars of degree at most k needed to cover the vertex set is n/(k+1) for a tournament of order n >= 14k log k" (bounded domination number, different problem).
- Conclusion: still no lower bound for f(4) beyond 47 in the 2004 literature; the Fisher computations say circulant S_4 needs >= 67 vertices.

### O. Javier, Llano, Zuazua, "2- and 3-existentially closed tournaments", Contrib. Discrete Math. 21(1) (2026) 73-90, DOI 10.55016/0sre1423 (received 12 Nov 2024). READ locally from https://cdm.ucalgary.ca/article/download/78881/58831/277078 (full text; key passages extracted by pdftotext).  THIS IS THE MOST RELEVANT PEER-REVIEWED PAPER ON 3-DOMINATOR PROPERTIES OF PALEY/DRT TOURNAMENTS.
Convention there: S_k means every k-set A has x not in A that is DOMINATED by every element of A (our S_k read in the converse tournament; for DRT/Paley the converse is isomorphic, so no matter). P_k = k-existentially closed: for each k-set A and each B subset of A there is x not in A dominating B and dominated by A\B.
- Abstract (verbatim sense): "It is proved that in vertex-transitive doubly regular tournaments properties S_3 and P_3 are equivalent and consequently the Paley tournament QR_p has property P_3 for every p = 3 mod 4 with p >= 19. ... the out- and in-neighborhood of every vertex of QR_p has property S_3 if and only if QR_p has property S_4 and that QR_67 has property S_4. ... the infinite families of Szekeres tournaments and their converses satisfy property P_3."
- Theorem 4.1 (JLZ): Let T be a doubly regular tournament, |V(T)| = 4n+3, n >= 4, {a,b,c} a triple, q = |N+(a) ∩ N+(b) ∩ N+(c)|. Then T has P_3 iff for every triple: (i) if T[a,b,c] is the cyclic triangle then q in [1, n-1]; (ii) if T[a,b,c] is transitive then q in [1, n-2]. ("Observe that 1 <= q <= n"; the lower bound 1 is the S_3 condition.)  For DRT(51): n = 12, so P_3 iff cyclic triples have 1 <= q <= 11 and transitive triples 1 <= q <= 10.  [Compare [52] (M1): k + |D+| = 12 - sigma: q in [..] consistent.]
- Corollary 4.2: a vertex-transitive DRT has P_3 iff it has S_3. Corollary 4.3: QR_p has P_3 for all p = 3 mod 4, p >= 19 (uses f(3)=19 and Graham-Spencer p > 9*2^4 = 144 for the rest + computation... they assert it from QR_19 having S_3 "and the main result of [6]").
- Quoted from Bonato-Cameron (Discrete Math 306 (2006) 2327-2335): if T has P_k (k>=2) then T[N+(v)], T[N-(v)] have P_{k-1}; "in particular if T has S_4 then T[N+(v)] and T[N-(v)] have S_3" (this is the one-step Szekeres descent; our [EP] Lemma 1.x uses it).
- Proposition 4.8: QR_p[N+(0)] (resp. N-(0)) has S_3 iff QR_p has S_4 (arc-transitivity). Corollary 4.11 gives the first PROOF in the literature that QR_67 has S_4: QR_67[N+(0)] = circulant C_33(K), K = {1,2,4,5,7,14,15,16,20,21,22,23,24,25,27,30} in Z_33, and S_3 follows from K - K = Z_33 (Prop. 4.10). [Graham-Spencer had stated it without proof.]
- Bonato-Gordinowicz-Pralat (as cited in JLZ): "there is a unique minimum order 3-e.c. tournament of order 19 and no 3-e.c. tournaments of orders 20,21,22 [this is about P_3, a stronger property]; there are no 4-e.c. tournaments of orders 47 and 48, improving the lower bound for the minimum order of such a tournament, and they verified that QR_67 is the smallest order Paley tournament that has property P_4." (n-e.c. = strong S_n: lower bounds for P_4 are NOT lower bounds for S_4 since P_4 => S_4 only in the wrong direction; but our f(4)>=52 would imply no P_4 tournament below 52 either, improving on 49.)  
- Open problems: 5.6 "Is it true that a vertex-transitive DRT has P_k iff it has S_k?"; 5.7 characterise circulant tournaments with P_2, P_3.

### R. Hand check of Wilder's N=47 argument (my own arithmetic; the argument is a model for N=51/N=52 work)
Setting: tournament on 47 vertices, every triple has c >= 5 dominators (F1). d^-(v) >= 23 (sum of d^- = C(47,2) = 1081 = 47*23, so all d^- = 23, regular). Every pair has |D(P)| >= 11; and |N+(w) ∩ N+(w')| = |D({w,w'})| + 46 - 23 - 23 = |D|, with sum over pairs of |D| = sum_w C(23,2) = 47*253 = 11891 = 1081 * 11, so every pair has |D(P)| = 11 exactly (since each >= 11). Then c(Y) <= |D(pair)| = 11, so 5 <= c <= 11.
First moment: sum_Y c = 47*C(23,3) = 47*1771 = 83237 (1771*47 = 70840+12397). Number of triples C(47,3) = 16215. Second: sum_Y C(c,2) = sum over pairs {w,w'} C(|N+(w)∩N+(w')|,3) = 1081*C(11,3) = 1081*165 = 178365, so sum c^2 = 2*178365 + 83237 = 439967. 
Then sum (c-5)(11-c) = -sum c^2 + 16 sum c - 55*16215 = -439967 + 1331792 - 891825 = 0. Each term >= 0, so c in {5,11} for every triple. Then sum (c-5) = 83237 - 81075 = 2162 is a multiple of 6; 2162 = 6*360 + 2. Contradiction. So no F1-tournament on 47 vertices => f(4) >= 48. CORRECT (this is an N=47 twin of the DRT(51) case: there everything is forced by regularity + codegree 12).

### (1) What is known (exact statements, all from sources read above)
1. Erdos 1963 (Math. Gaz. 47, 220-223): f(k) >= 2^{k+1}-1; f(k) <= (ln 2 + o(1)) k^2 2^k.  [quoted in Bozoki, Jeffries, Simon, Lee]
2. Szekeres & Szekeres 1965 (Math. Gaz. 49, 290-293): f(k) >= (k+2)2^{k-1}-1 for k > 2; f(3)=19 (f(1)=3, f(2)=7 are Schutte/Erdos).  Hence f(4) >= 47, f(5) >= 111.
3. Graham & Spencer 1971: QR_p has S_k if p > k^2 2^{2k-2}; QR_7, QR_19, QR_67 have S_2, S_3, S_4 (QR_67 first proved in JLZ 2026, Cor. 4.11, in a short argument: K-K = Z_33); "it is possible that T_67 is also minimal" (their only comment on f(4)).
4. Fisher 1996 (private communication, reported in Reid et al 2004 and Bozoki 2014; Bozoki reproduced the S_4 and S_5 cases): the smallest ROTATIONAL (circulant) tournament with S_4 has 67 vertices; smallest Paley with S_5, S_6, S_7 have 331, 1163... So f(4) <= 67, and for circulant tournaments equality.
5. Bonato-Gordinowicz-Pralat (Contrib. Discrete Math. 5 (2010) 52-66, computer): no 4-e.c. (strong P_4) tournaments of orders 47, 48; unique 3-e.c. of minimum order 19.
6. Wilder (GitHub, Lean, Aug-Sep 2026; unrefereed but machine-checked claims): f(4) >= 48 via an N=47 two-moment argument; computer-assisted ("McKay catalogue") attempt at f(4) >= 49. Nothing above 48 is claimed as proved.

### (3) Techniques from the literature that might help
(a) Wilder's N=47 argument (Erdos902Reid.lean): regularity forced (all in-degrees = 23), all pair codegrees exactly 11, then sum (c-5)(11-c) = 0 for the triple dominator counts c, forcing c in {5,11} and a divisibility contradiction mod 6 (2162 not divisible by 6). This is the N=47 twin of our DRT(51) positivity trick, and works because at N = 47 everything is rigid. [Arithmetic re-checked above.] It suggests, for N=52, looking for a similar polynomial identity that forces c in two values {5, 5+m} and then a divisibility/parity obstruction -- but at N=52 the lack of regularity (sum e = 26) and L_Y variable (BRIEF section 9) destroy the rigidity, as already found.
(b) JLZ Theorem 4.1 (DRT of order 4n+3: P_3 iff triple intersection q lies in [1,n-1] for cyclic, [1,n-2] for transitive triples): gives a clean S_3-in-DRT criterion. Combined with Prop. 4.8 (QR_p has S_4 iff its neighbourhoods have S_3) it settles Paley cases via the sumset condition K-K = Z_33; useful for DRT(51)-type local analysis ((M1)-(M4) in the brief), not for N=52 (no vertex-transitivity).
(c) The Bonato-Cameron lemma (P_k => neighbourhoods have P_{k-1}) = the one-step descent that gives d^-(v) >= 23 etc. (already in [EP]).
(d) Nothing in the literature on T2: all lower bounds are first/second-moment counting plus descent, all upper bounds probabilistic; the "barrier" results in the brief (counting at one level cannot beat ~0.77 n 2^n) have no counter-example in the literature. A genuinely new idea would be needed (e.g. VC-dimension/epsilon-net lower bounds do not apply since S_k is a covering, not shattering, property).
