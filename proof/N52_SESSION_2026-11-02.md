## S7. Fourier form of k and u (PROVED, any tournament on 52 vertices)

For a triple Y = {y1, y2, y3} put tau(Y) := sum_{w not in Y} M_{w y1} M_{w y2} M_{w y3} (a sum of 49 signs, so tau is odd).
Expanding k = sum_w prod_i (1 + M_{w y_i})/2 and l = sum_w prod_i (1 - M_{w y_i})/2 over the 49 outside w:
- linear part: sum_i sum_{w not in Y} M_{w y_i} = sum_i (d-(y_i) - d+(y_i)) = sum_i (2 e_i - 1) (the arcs inside Y cancel);
- quadratic part: sum_{i<j} [ (M M^T)_{y_i y_j} - M_{y_i y_k} M_{y_j y_k} ], and sum over the three pairs of the subtracted sign is
  (sources and sinks) - (middles) = 2 - 1 = 1 for a transitive triple and 0 - 3 = -3 for a cyclic one, i.e. 4 sigma - 3;
- with (M M^T)_{vw} = 4 x_vw - 2(e_v + e_w) (S6, D = 12 + x): the quadratic part is 4 X_Y - 4 sum e - 4 sigma + 3.
Hence, for every triple,
  **8 k(Y) = 49 + 4 X_Y - 2 E(Y) - 4 sigma(Y) + tau(Y),   8 l(Y) = 55 + 4 X_Y - 6 E(Y) - 4 sigma(Y) - tau(Y),**
where E(Y) = sum_{y in Y} e(y). Checks: L = k + l = 13 - E(Y) - sigma + X_Y (the known L-identity); and
  **u_Y = (k - l)/2 = (tau(Y) + 2 E(Y) - 3)/8.**
Integrality: tau = -1 + 2E + 4 sigma + 4X mod 8. For Y inside V_0 (balanced Case A): tau = 3 mod 8 (transitive), 7 mod 8 (cyclic),
and F1 (k >= 5) reads tau >= -5 (transitive), tau >= -9 (cyclic).
Second moment: sum_Y tau^2 = 52 C(51,3) + (1/3) sum_pairs E_P (E_P^2 - 148), where E_P = (M M^T)_P (because the 50 signs
M_{wy} M_{w'y} have sum E_{ww'} and their third elementary symmetric function is s(s^2 - 148)/6). In balanced Case A this gives
mean tau^2 about 51.8 (std of u about 0.9), matching Var k ~ 0.95. So F1 truncates tau at about -0.7 standard deviations; the cubic
identity becomes sum u^3 + (3/4) sum L^2 u + 3 eta_T = 0 (using sum u = 0, sum L u = -eta_T), so the strong right skew of u that F1
forces must be balanced by **sum L^2 u < 0: triples with large L must have k < l.** This is the precise mechanism by which defects
(which raise L) rescue the balanced case, and it is what a proof has to break.
Bessel bound from (TF): for i != j in V_0, sum_{k in V_0} tau(ijk)^2 <= 51 * 50 (the vector w -> M_iw M_jw has 50 nonzero entries).
Typical value is about 1275, so this is weak. DEAD END as stated.

## S8. The "twin" sub-family of S5 (PROVED reduction)

*Proof.* Use the certificate of S5: with u = k - 11/2 (transitive) or u = k - 6 (cyclic),
phi_T(u) = -1/4 + u^2 - (2/3)(u^3 - u/4), phi_C(u) = -1 + u^2 - (2/3)(u^3 - u). By [52] (M2)-(M4) and (M3) in the form
sum u^2 = 16575 (S5), summing over all 20825 triples gives exactly **sum phi = -15300/4 - 5525 + 16575 = 7225.**
Values (rechecked): transitive k = 4..11: phi = 4, 0, 0, 0, -4, -16, -40, -80; cyclic k = 4..12: phi = 7, 0, -1, 0, -1, -8, -25, -56, -105.
So phi <= 0 on every triple with k >= 5 ("good"), and phi = 4 or 7 on the bad ones (k = 4), all of which lie inside S.
Let B_T, B_C be the bad transitive and cyclic triples, and t = T_S - B_T, c = C_S - B_C the good ones inside S. Then
  7225 = sum phi <= 4 B_T + 7 B_C - sum_{good inside S} |phi|,
i.e. with cost(k) := 4 + |phi_T(k)| (transitive) or 7 + |phi_C(k)| (cyclic),
  **sum_{good Y inside S} cost(k(Y)) <= 4 T_S + 7 C_S - 7225.**                                    (C1)
On the other hand bad triples have k - 4 = 0, so
  **sum_{good Y inside S} (k(Y) - 4) = K_S - 4 C(s,3).**                                            (C2)
Pointwise, k - 4 <= (3/4) cost(k) for every good triple. Table (k - 4 versus cost):
transitive k = 5..11: (1,4), (2,4), (3,4), (4,8), (5,20), (6,44), (7,84); cyclic k = 5..12: (1,7), (2,8), (3,7), (4,8), (5,15),
(6,32), (7,63), (8,112). The largest ratio is 3/4, at transitive k = 7. Combining (C1) and (C2):
  K_S - 4 C(s,3) <= (3/4)(4 T_S + 7 C_S - 7225) = 3 T_S + (21/4) C_S - 5418.75.  ∎

(This is the same certificate that proves Theorem 5 of [52]; the new point is that it charges every good triple inside S, and a
region that has to absorb all the bad triples also has to carry a large total k.)

## S11. No F1-tournament on 52 vertices is a DRT(51) plus one vertex in Case A (PROVED)

**Setting.** T is an F1-tournament on 52 vertices, x a vertex, and T' = T - x is a DRT(51). Put S = N+(x) (in T), s = |S|,
W = V(T') \ S. A triple of T' gains the dominator x exactly when it lies inside S. So F1 for T says: triples of T' not inside S
have >= 5 dominators in T', triples inside S have >= 4. Lemma C applies.
The degrees of T are d-(v) = 25 + [v in S] for v != x and d-(x) = 51 - s, so e(v) = [v in S], e(x) = 26 - s.
For v in W, D_T({v,x}) = N-_T(v) ∩ W, the in-neighbourhood of v inside T'[W].

**Case s = 26 (x in V_0, the S5 family), no tight pair.** Then |W| = 25 and the in-degrees inside T'[W] are >= 12 (no tight pair)
and add up to C(25,2) = 300, so T'[W] is 12-regular. As computed in S5: every z in W beats exactly 13 vertices of S; for b in S
let f(b) be its out-degree inside S; then sum f = 325, sum f^2 = 4225 (so sum (f - 12.5)^2 = 162.5), T_S = 1950, C_S = 650.
- K_S = sum_{z in W} C(13,3) + sum_{b in S} C(f(b),3) = 7150 + sum_b C(f(b),3).
- With d = f - 12.5: C(f,3) = [1509.375 + 395.75 d + 34.5 d^2 + d^3]/6, so sum_b C(f,3) = [26*1509.375 + 34.5*162.5 + sum d^3]/6
  = [44850 + sum d^3]/6. Since f >= 0, d >= -12.5, so d^3 >= -12.5 d^2 when d < 0, and **sum d^3 >= -12.5*162.5 = -2031.25.**
  Hence sum_b C(f,3) >= 42818.75/6 = 7136.46 and **K_S >= 14286.46.**
- Lemma C: K_S <= 4*2600 + 3*1950 + (21/4)*650 - 5418.75 = 10400 + 5850 + 3412.5 - 5418.75 = **14243.75.**
Contradiction (margin 42.7).

**Case s = 25 (x in V_1 beating all of V_1 \ {x}), balanced, no tight pair.** Then S = V_1 \ {x}, W = V_0, |W| = 26.
For v in W the in-degree inside T'[W] is D_T(v,x) = 12 + x_v with x_v := x(v,x) >= 0 and sum_v x_v = 13 (the in-weight of x in V_1).
Put q = sum x_v^2 (13 <= q <= 169). For b in S let f(b) = out-degree inside S (= |N+(b) ∩ V_1| in T, since x -> b), so sum f = 300.
- Pairs inside W: sum_{pairs in W} c+_W = sum_{u in W} C(12 + x_u, 2) = 26*66 + 23*13/2 + q/2 = 1865.5 + q/2.
- For b in S, |N-(b) ∩ W| = 1 + f(b) (b has out-degree 25 in T', of which f(b) inside S). Counting pairs of W below b:
  sum_b C(1 + f(b), 2) = sum_{pairs in W} (c+_T'(v,v') - c+_W(v,v')) = 12*325 - 1865.5 - q/2 = 2034.5 - q/2.
  So sum f^2 = 4069 - q - 300 = 3769 - q, and with d = f - 12: sum d = 0, **sum d^2 = 169 - q.**
- T_S = sum_b C(f,2) = (3769 - q - 300)/2 = 1734.5 - q/2, C_S = 2300 - T_S.
- For v in W, |N+(v) ∩ S| = 25 - (out-degree inside W) = in-degree inside W = 12 + x_v. So
  K_S = sum_b C(f,3) + sum_v C(12 + x_v, 3). With C(12+y,3) = [1320 + 362 y + 33 y^2 + y^3]/6:
  sum_b C(f,3) = [33000 + 33(169 - q) + sum d^3]/6, sum_v C(12 + x_v,3) = [34320 + 362*13 + 33 q + sum x^3]/6,
  so **K_S = [77603 + sum d^3 + sum x_v^3]/6.**
- Lemma C: K_S <= 4*2300 + 3 T_S + (21/4)(2300 - T_S) - 5418.75 = 15856.25 - 2.25 T_S = 11953.625 + 1.125 q.
- So we would need sum d^3 + sum x^3 <= -5881.25 + 6.75 q. But d >= -12 gives sum d^3 >= -12(169 - q), and x^3 >= x^2 gives
  sum x^3 >= q, so sum d^3 + sum x^3 >= -2028 + 13 q > -5881.25 + 6.75 q. Contradiction (margin >= 3853 + 6.25 q).

**Theorem S11.** In balanced Case A (degrees 25/26, no tight pair), T - x is never a DRT(51). In particular the twin family of S8

## S12. Balanced Case A with u_x = 13 forces twins; the non-DRT twin case (PROVED reduction; remaining case OPEN)

Let x in V_0 beat all of V_1 (u_x = 13). Then D0 = 162.5, so D1 = 162.5 (NT-B (d)). For b in V_1, D_T(x,b) = |N-(x) ∩ N-(b)| =
|W ∩ N-(b)| = f(b), where W = V_0 \ {x}; with no tight pair, f(b) >= 12 for all b. Since sum_{V_1} (f - 12) = 13 and
sum (f - 12.5)^2 = 162.5, we get sum (f - 12)^2 = 169 with all terms >= 0, so **one vertex b0 has f(b0) = 25 and every other
vertex of V_1 has f = 12.** Then b0 beats V_1 \ {b0}, its 26 in-neighbours are exactly V_0 (including x), so x and b0 have the same
relation to every other vertex: **x and b0 are twins** (x -> b0). T - x is regular, and it is a DRT(51) iff every pair inside V_1
has x_P = 1. If every x_P <= 1 on HH pairs then all are 1 (they sum to 325 over 325 pairs) and S11 applies. So the only survivor
of u_x = 13 is the twin family with T - x regular but not doubly regular: some HH pair has x_P = 0 (codegree 11 in T - x) and
some has x_P >= 2. In T - x the class sums of u still vanish (regular tournament: sum over transitive triples of (k - l) =
sum_P [C(12 + g_P, 2) - C(12 - g_P, 2)] = 23 sum g = 0, with g = D_{T-x} - 12), but L = 12 - sigma + (sum of g over the triple)
varies on triples with a pair inside V_1, so Lemma C picks up error terms there. OPEN.

## S13. Exact certificate identity for every regular tournament on 51 vertices (PROVED)

Let T' be any regular tournament on 51 vertices, g_P := D(P) - 12 (so sum g = 0, Gamma2 := sum g^2, Gamma3 := sum g^3), and for a
triple Y put G_Y = sum of g over its three pairs, v_Y := k - (12 - sigma)/2, and phi the S5 certificate evaluated at v
(so phi depends only on k and sigma). Then
  **sum_Y phi(v_Y) = 7225 + 6.5 Gamma2 + Gamma3/3 - 2 sum_Y [C(l_Y, 3) - C(l_Y - G_Y, 3)]   (R51)**
     = 7225 + 11.25 Gamma2 + Gamma3/3 - (2/3) sum_Y v_Y^3.
*Proof.* Regular on 51 vertices gives c+ = D on every pair and L = 12 - sigma + G. Transitive triples inside N+(w) are counted by
C(c+(w,b),2), so sum over transitive Y of k = sum_P C(12 + g,2) = 84150 + Gamma2/2, and likewise sum over transitive Y of l =
sum_P C(12 - g, 2) = 84150 + Gamma2/2; hence sum_T v = Gamma2/2, sum_C v = -Gamma2/2. sum C(k,2) = sum_P C(12 + g,3) =
280500 + (33 Gamma2 + Gamma3)/6, which gives sum v^2 = 16575 + 11.5 Gamma2 + Gamma3/3 (hand: 678300 - 925650 - 397800 + 661725 =
16575). For the cube, with a = (12 - sigma)/2 - 1 and f(t) = t^3 - t, 6[C(k,3) - C(12 - sigma - k, 3)] = 2 v^3 + 2(3a^2 - 1) v;
summing and using sum C(k,3) = sum C(l,3) and 3a^2 - 1 = 59.75 (transitive), 74 (cyclic) gives
2 sum v^3 = 6 sum [C(l,3) - C(l - G,3)] + 14.25 Gamma2. Substituting into
sum phi = -3825 - 5525 + sum v^2 - (2/3) sum v^3 + (1/6) sum_T v + (2/3) sum_C v gives (R51).  ∎
For a DRT (g = 0) this is the 7225 of S5.

**The regular twin family (survivor of S12) in these terms.** There T' = T - x is regular, the bad region is B = N+(b0),
K_B = 13300, T_B = 1650, C_B = 650 exactly as in the DRT twin case, and g lives only on the arcs of T'[B] (with zero in- and
out-sums at every vertex). Lemma C with (R51) in place of 7225 shows the family survives only if
  **sum_Y v_Y^3 >= 2312.5 + 16.875 Gamma2 + Gamma3/2,**
i.e. the k-distribution of T' must be strongly right-skewed (it is 0 in a DRT). A minimal perturbation has Gamma2 = 4
(g = +1, +1, -1, -1 on a 4-cycle of arcs); a first-order estimate of the correction (each pair with g = -1 lies in 49 triples,
with l about 5.7) suggests that Gamma2 >= 7 or so is needed. Not decided.

## S9. Where this leaves T1 (assessment, 2 Nov 2026)
